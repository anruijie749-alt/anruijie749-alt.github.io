What this wheel actually does

It picks one lunch option for you, and that is the whole point. When you already know roughly what you can accept, deciding is not a matter of taste, it is a matter of stopping the comparison. The wheel does the stopping.

How to use it in three steps

  • Set a budget first. A range removes about half of the options before you look at a single menu.
  • Pick a flavour profile: mild, medium, spicy, or vegetarian. That is elimination, not preference.
  • Spin once and go with the result. If you spin more than four times, you already know what you want.

Why a random pick works for lunch

Most lunch choices are close to equivalent. Rice or noodles, this shop or the next, over a month the difference is small. What is not small is the ten minutes spent scrolling and the low mood that comes with it. Handing that decision to a wheel costs nothing and gives the time back.

Where to draw the line: low-cost decisions such as lunch, what to wear, which film to watch, let the wheel decide. High-cost decisions such as housing, jobs, health, take your time and gather information instead.

Privacy and technical notes

No account is needed, nothing is uploaded, and the page works offline once loaded. Recent picks are stored only in your own browser, and clearing site data removes them. There is no location request and no automatic redirect. Any link to a delivery platform opens only when you tap it yourself.

Frequently asked

Is the result truly random?

It is picked from the options that match the filters you set, so it is random within your own limits rather than across every dish that exists.

Can I use it on a phone?

Yes. The buttons are large and the layout fits a narrow screen, so it works one-handed.

Does it remember my history?

Only inside your own browser, and only the last few picks. Nothing is sent to a server.
